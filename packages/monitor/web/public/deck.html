The tool

stoke: two engines behind one command.

Claude keeps your conversation cached for a few minutes. Idle past that and the next message pays to rebuild all of it at full price.

Engine 1, prevent

Keep-alive proxy

Sits in front of the API and sends a zero-output ping before the cache window closes, so the conversation stays warm while you are away.

Engine 2, measure

Monitor

Reads every transcript into SQLite, prices each turn, and runs five detectors that name the avoidable charges. Six hooks act on them live, including a hard block on oversized sessions.

What worked

Measurement changed my behaviour more than automation did.

  • Naming the cause in plain language."You walked away and the cache went cold" is something I can act on. A row reading cache_expiry is not.
  • Blocking oversized sessions outright.The only lever with a certain payoff. It refuses the message instead of suggesting something, and it has fired on real work.
  • Making the readout cheap to load.The dashboard went from 437 KB and 66,000 pixels of scroll to 2 KB. One query went from 277 ms to 0.03 ms.
What did not work

The thing I got wrong was my own reading of the data.

  • I almost cut the one part that was making money. Most ping spend goes to sessions that never come back, which reads like waste. It is not. Every tighter cutoff I simulated lost money, because a rebuild avoided is worth many times what a ping costs.
  • My first dashboard was unreadable.Over a thousand rows, and the biggest number on screen was a negative amount. I had built it for myself, not for anyone seeing it once.
  • One promised view was impossible.A before and after install comparison. stoke started logging before my earliest transcript, so there is no before. I withdrew it rather than fake it.
What I would do differently

Price the counterfactual, not just the cost.

  • What something costs is easy. What it bought is the real question.The ping spend took an hour to total. Working out what it prevented took a simulation over every idle session, and it reversed my conclusion.
  • Judge a bet by its odds, not by its losing half.Paying cents for a coin-flip at a dollar is a good trade. Counting only the losses makes it look like a leak.
  • Test against live data, not fixtures.Two bugs passed every unit test and only appeared against the real database.
  • Let the tool measure itself.Every figure in this deck is read live from stoke's own database, including the one that proved me wrong.
